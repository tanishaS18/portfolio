# **Study Circle — Finding Your Study Spot**

## **Overview**

**Find your spot. Find your people.**

Study Circle is a mobile app that helps UW students find study spots that fit how they like to study, and see where their friends are studying.

## **Problem**

Students waste time hunting for a place to study, and often settle for a spot that doesn't suit them. A library might be packed, a café might have no outlets, and the only way to find out is to walk there.

**Problem statement:** UW students lack an easy way to find study spots that match their needs, such as crowd level, outlets, food, and noise, before they commit to going there.

## **Solution**

Study Circle matches students to study spots based on their preferences, and shows what each spot is like right now.

* **Personalized filters** for crowd level, ratings, outlets, food options, distance, price, and study environment.  
* **Map view** to see nearby spots at a glance and navigate to them.  
* **Live spot details** such as current busyness and student reviews.  
* **Friends and feed** to see where friends are studying, share recommendations, and send study session invites.  
* **Accessibility built in**, including screen reader support and wheelchair-accessible routes.

## **Market analysis**

Students already use general tools to find places, but none are built for studying. We compared three of them:

| Tool | What it does well | What it misses for studying |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Google Maps | Map of nearby places, directions, busy-time estimates | No study-specific info like outlets, noise, or seating |
| Yelp | Detailed reviews and ratings | Reviews focus on food and service, not studying |
| UW app | Campus buildings and library hours | No crowd levels, filters, or reviews of spaces |

**The gap:** no tool combined a map, study-specific filters, and live crowd info in one place, and none had a social layer for studying with friends.

## **User testing**

We ran 1-on-1 usability tests with 8 UW students studying on campus, followed by short interviews. Participants were split evenly: 4 peers and 4 students we approached at random while they were studying.

### **How we tested**

1. Introduced the problem and walked through Study Circle and its features.  
2. Asked participants to complete specific tasks in the prototype while thinking aloud.  
3. Followed up with a post-test interview to uncover what they were thinking but didn't say during the tasks.

### **What worked**

* The **map view** made it easy to locate spots.  
* The **filters** were seen as useful and well designed.  
* The **review system** was well received.  
* Participants liked the idea of a **friend system** to make the app more engaging.

## **Final design**

The final prototype brings together study-specific filters, a live map, and a social layer, so students can decide where to study before they leave.

* **Home and map.** Nearby spots shown together on the map, filtered to the student's preferences.  
* **Spot details.** Live busyness, ratings, outlets, food, and reviews in one view.  
* **Saved spots.** Higher-contrast cards with clear indicators.  
* **Friends and feed.** See where friends are studying, browse their recommendations, and send study session invites.
